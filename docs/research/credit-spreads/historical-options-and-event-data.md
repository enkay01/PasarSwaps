# Historical options and event data for credit-spread research

Status: research finding
Evidence checked: 2026-08-23

## The answer

Several current providers can supply enough US equity and ETF option data for a credit-spread backtest, but no public product description proves that one product supplies every required fact with point-in-time eligibility. There are two practical paths. You can buy a prepared chain dataset with bid and ask quotes, open interest, implied volatility, Greeks, underlying prices, and reference data, or buy raw or normalized OPRA data and join point-in-time contract definitions, underlying prices, dividends, interest rates, CorporateActions, and earnings-calendar vintages yourself, calculating implied volatility and Greeks locally. End-of-day data can test entry selection and exits evaluated once per day. It cannot test an intraday stop rule faithfully. One-minute snapshots can test a bar-based stop approximation. Tick quotes can test quote-triggered stops, but the Execution Model still has to define latency, leg order, partial fills, and slippage. None of these datasets reports the assignment that would have happened to a simulated short option, so the lab has to model exercise and assignment from contract terms, moneyness, time, ex-dividend facts, and an explicit assignment rule.

## Vendor notes

### OptionMetrics IvyDB US

OptionMetrics has the longest documented prepared end-of-day history in this review. [IvyDB US](https://optionmetrics.com/data-products/) covers US exchange-traded equity and index options, including ETF and ADR options, from January 1996, with closing bid and ask quotes, volume, open interest, underlying prices, interest rates, dividends, CorporateActions, implied volatility, and delta, gamma, vega, and theta, plus permanent identifiers, delisted names, and daily patch files. [IvyDB US Intraday](https://optionmetrics.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/OM_IvyDB-US_Intraday_Flyer_WEB.pdf) starts in 2018 with synchronized cuts at 10:00, 14:00, and 15:45 ET that carry bid and ask quotes, underlying prices, volume, implied volatility, and Greeks, but the public flyer does not list open interest for this product.

### Cboe DataShop

[Option EOD Summary](https://datashop.cboe.com/option-eod-summary) supplies a 15:45 ET snapshot and an end-of-day snapshot for US stock, ETF, and index options, with NBBO bid and ask and sizes, underlying bid and ask for stocks and ETFs, option OHLC, volume, VWAP, open interest, and optional implied volatility and Greeks. The product page states history from January 2012 while the [DataShop FAQ](https://datashop.cboe.com/faqs) says January 2010, and a purchase should resolve that conflict before use. [Option Quotes](https://datashop.cboe.com/option-quote-intervals) supplies one-minute or custom interval NBBO snapshots from January 2012, and [Option Trades](https://datashop.cboe.com/option-trades) is a separate product with trade price, size, execution exchange, NBBO at trade time, underlying bid and ask, condition mappings, and optional trade Greeks.

### ORATS

[ORATS EOD API](https://orats.com/data-api) supplies near-close chains from about 14 minutes before the close with history from 2007, including strike and expiration, call and put bid and ask, sizes, volume, open interest, stock price, bid, mid, and ask implied volatility, theoretical values, and Greeks. [ORATS Intraday API](https://orats.com/intraday-data-api) supplies one-minute chain snapshots from August 2020 across more than 5,000 symbols, and the company also offers [physical-drive delivery](https://orats.com/hard-drive-delivery) of gzip CSV files with SHA-256 manifests. The [earnings-history endpoint](https://orats.com/docs/historical-data-api) supplies an earnings date, a before, after, during, or unknown time-of-day code, and an update timestamp.

### ThetaData

[ThetaData options coverage](https://www.thetadata.net/options-data) states that it has every OPRA trade and NBBO quote, daily open interest, tick, one-second, one-minute, and end-of-day output, with history from June 2012, served through a local Theta Terminal that exports CSV, JSON, or NDJSON. The [quote endpoint](https://docs.thetadata.us/operations/option_history_quote.html) returns timestamped NBBO price, size, exchange, and condition data for a contract or full chain, the [trade endpoint](https://docs.thetadata.us/operations/option_history_trade.html) returns every OPRA trade, and Greeks endpoints calculate first-, second-, and third-order values from time-aligned option and underlying midpoints. The public schema identifies a contract by underlying symbol, expiration, strike, and right, with no documented permanent contract identifier, adjusted deliverables, exercise or settlement style, delisted-underlying coverage, CorporateActions, earnings events, or correction history.

### Massive

[Massive option flat files](https://massive.com/docs/flat-files/options/overview) provide daily CSV files through an S3-compatible interface, with minute and daily aggregates and trades back to June 2014 and [tick quote files](https://massive.com/docs/flat-files/options/quotes) from 2022-03-07 using nanosecond timestamps. The [contract reference endpoint](https://massive.com/docs/rest/options/contracts) includes active and expired contracts, an as-of date filter, OSI-like tickers, correction number, call or put, strike, expiration, exercise style, shares per contract, exchange, and additional deliverables, and Massive states that it carries OCC and OPRA contract changes after CorporateActions. Historical flat files do not contain Greeks, implied volatility, or open interest, and the snapshot endpoints expose current derived values and daily open interest without a documented historical point-in-time series.

### Databento OPRA.PILLAR

[Databento OPRA.PILLAR](https://databento.com/datasets/OPRA.PILLAR) covers 18 US option venues and advertises availability from 2013-04-01, with history varying by schema, and supplies trades, consolidated NBBO at tick, one-second, and one-minute cadences, OHLCV, statistics, status, and point-in-time definitions. Raw symbols use OSI symbology, parent symbology can fetch an entire chain, and definitions are timestamped with strike, expiration, activation, contract class, and add, modify, or delete actions, though instrument IDs are only guaranteed unique within one day. The [2025 normalization notice](https://databento.com/blog/opra-migration) states that CMBP-1 and TCBBO history starts on 2023-03-28, and Databento does not publish option implied volatility or Greeks in the OPRA schemas.

### algoseek US Options

[algoseek US Options](https://algoseek.com/options/) has OPRA trades, NBBO quotes, minute bars, open interest, daily Greeks, and reference data, with the full trade and quote archive starting in 2014 and expired contracts preserved for survivorship-bias-free research. The [options contract security master](https://algoseek.com/docs/rest-api/reference/us-equity-opt-ref-sec-master-lookups) covers listed and delisted contracts from 2007 with root and underlying symbols, full contract ticker, call or put, strike, expiration, trading dates, settlement type, and adjusted-deliverable data, and detailed settlement coverage starts in 2018. Daily option analytics use Black-Scholes-Merton for European options and a finite-difference method for American options.

## Reference detail

### What an import must preserve

A Dataset Version for this technique should preserve these fields, or record why they are absent:

- Option Contract identity: provider identifier, raw OSI or OPRA symbol, underlying Security identifier, call or put, strike, expiration, exercise style, settlement type, multiplier, deliverables, listing time, and inactivation time.
- Market and pricing observation: event time, provider receive time when available, trade price and size, bid and ask price and size, exchange or NBBO source, condition codes, volume, open interest effective date, time-aligned underlying bid and ask or trade, interest-rate input, dividend inputs, and any borrow or residual-yield input.
- Point-in-time and corporate facts: provider publication time, retrieval time, correction or revision identifier, the earliest time the Analyst could use the record, split, merger, spin-off, special dividend, symbol change, adjusted deliverable, OCC memo or equivalent, earnings expected date and time, before or after market classification, confirmation status, the time that version became known, later revisions, and the actual release time.
- Derived and provenance: implied volatility, Greeks, pricing model and model version, input timestamps, calculation status, provider, product, license class, query or file name, checksum, schema version, time zone, and import code version.

Each raw acquisition must stay immutable. A later vendor correction must create a new Dataset Version or a patch layer, and it must not alter a completed Backtest Run.

### Vendor prices and delivery

- OptionMetrics permits clients to host the database locally rather than access it only through a platform. Public pages do not state current prices, export limits, retention rights after termination, the full US file schema, or earnings-event coverage, so sales provides the quote and license terms ([data products](https://optionmetrics.com/data-products/)).
- Cboe files arrive as CSV over SFTP and are removed from the folder after 30 days, so the lab must archive and checksum each file locally. The purchase form prices symbols, dates, cadence, calculations, open interest, customer type, and distribution rights, and no stable public bundle price applies. Cboe estimates one year of full-market one-minute data at about 2.3 TB compressed with calculations, or 1.2 TB without them, and notes that a digit in the option root identifies many non-standard contracts after a CorporateAction without describing the deliverable.
- The ORATS individual delayed API tier is $99 per month with 20,000 requests and documents dividend, earnings, split, and IV-rank history; the individual live intraday tier is $399 per month with 1,000,000 requests; and physical-drive delivery is $2,000 plus a refundable $1,000 drive deposit for the one-minute archive from October 2020, with the 2015 to September 2020 two-minute archive as a $1,000 add-on. The [one-minute README](https://orats.com/readme-1min.pdf) explains timestamp range changes and a historical index-derived-price issue, where some old index Greeks and implied volatilities should only be used at specified five-minute timestamps, and the [two-minute README](https://orats.com/readme-2min.pdf) includes an errata file for missing days, missing minutes, and low ticker counts. The public docs do not show the historical versions of expected earnings dates that existed before each entry decision, and they do not prove full delisted-symbol coverage, permanent identifiers through mergers, adjusted deliverables, exercise style, a correction replacement policy, or API retention rights. The hard-drive page says the purchase includes a data license, and the OPRA delayed agreement prohibits redistribution outside the subscriber's organization.
- ThetaData open interest is normally published once per day around 06:30 ET and is the prior day's close. Current individual option plans are $40, $80, and $160 per month for four, eight, and twelve years of history, tick data starts at $80, and current commercial options pricing is $1,600 per month billed annually, with startup discounts offered through sales.
- Massive individual options plans cost $0, $29, $79, and $199 per month, and only the $199 tier includes historical tick quotes; individual plans restrict use to the individual. The business options plan is $1,999 per month and documents more than ten years of trades but only about 2.5 years of quotes. The documented 2025 quote files total about 30.1 TB. Two $99 per month expansions add event data: [Benzinga Earnings](https://massive.com/docs/rest/partners/benzinga/earnings) has dates, reported or scheduled times, confirmation status, last-updated timestamps, and history from 2010, and [TMX Corporate Events](https://massive.com/docs/rest/partners/tmx/corporate-events) has status and event metadata from 2018. Neither public endpoint documents a complete archive of every prior schedule version.
- Databento historical data uses per-GB pricing with $125 of initial credit, exact OPRA unit prices come from the authenticated catalogue or metadata API, an OPRA Standard plan at $199 per month covers live access while historical pay-as-you-go remains available, batch files expire after 30 days but can be downloaded again during that period, and licensing and redistribution rights depend on the dataset and selected use in the License Manager. The reviewed docs expose record quality flags and schema release notes but not a historical-restatement policy that would reproduce an old response from the API alone, so the lab must retain the bytes, metadata, schema version, and checksum.
- algoseek published prices are $2,000 per month for tick trades and NBBO quotes, $1,800 for minute bars, and $1,200 for open interest; the 15-dataset Options Research Package is $3,000 per month on a two-year term and covers history from 2014, with a sandbox of up to one year without a credit card, and historical subscriptions include the full archive and daily updates. A stable ASID exists at the option-root level, not necessarily for each strike and expiration, and the separate OCC Listed Options Daily and Special Settlements datasets add listed-series and adjusted-deliverable facts. Delivery supports CSV, SQL, and REST queries, and the pricing page says its displayed prices are samples, so a written quote remains necessary.

### Companion sources

- [Wall Street Horizon DateBreaks](https://www.wallstreethorizon.com/upload/WSHDatebreaksSales.pdf) advertises optional point-in-time earnings-date history back to 2006, including prior, current, forecast, confirmed, and revised dates. Price, delivery format, time-of-day history, export rights, and retention terms require a quote.
- [OCC product and series data](https://www.theocc.com/market-data/market-data-reports/other-market-data-info/data-sales) includes exercise style, classification, settlement method, underlying, activation and inactivation dates, expiration, strike, and listed exchanges, at $1,750 per month for the non-distribution price. OCC also publishes [daily open interest](https://www.theocc.com/market-data/market-data-reports/other-market-data-info/batch-processing/daily-open-interest) and [special-settlement and contract-adjustment memos](https://infomemo.theocc.com/infomemo/search), and public report retention varies, so those reports are not a substitute for a licensed historical contract master.
- [SEC EDGAR APIs](https://www.sec.gov/search-filings/edgar-application-programming-interfaces) provide free real-time filing dissemination metadata and nightly bulk files, which can verify when an 8-K or earnings exhibit became public. They do not provide a reliable future earnings schedule, and the filing acceptance time is not always the first public release time.

### Questions a pilot should answer

- Can the provider reproduce a full chain from a delisted Security and an adjusted contract, including its original deliverable and settlement method?
- Does the option quote timestamp represent exchange event time, provider receive time, interval end, or file publication time, and does an interval row carry the last quote, a time-weighted quote, or only a quote that changed inside the interval?
- Are zero volume and zero open interest true zeros or missing messages, which trade conditions count toward OHLC and volume, and are calculated Greeks reproducible from documented inputs, model family, dividends, rates, borrow assumptions, and rounding rules?
- Can the provider produce the original file and each later correction, and does a correction replace history or arrive as a patch?
- Does the license allow permanent local retention for internal Backtest Runs, derived values, checksums, backups, and use after cancellation, and can the lab export all selected symbols and dates without an undocumented row, request, or file limit?
- Does the earnings product store every schedule version with its known-at timestamp, confirmation state, and before or after market time, and what exact quote applies to a small pilot universe, a broad US equity and ETF universe, and each required cadence?

Do not judge a provider from AAPL and SPY alone. Request the same dates and symbols from each candidate: one liquid ETF with weeklies, one liquid single-name Security across an earnings release, one delisted Security, one split or merger that created a non-standard deliverable, one dividend-paying Security near its ex-dividend date, one half trading day, one zero-volume but quoted Option Contract, and one corrected or canceled trade if the source preserves it. Hash the raw files, import them into a temporary Dataset Version, and reconcile contract counts, NBBO, trades, volume, open interest, CorporateActions, and earnings-known-at times before running one EOD exit and one intraday stop scenario.

The lab also has to choose which data architecture it will support: a prepared-chain adapter that trusts and records vendor analytics, a raw-OPRA adapter with a local analytics pipeline, or both behind one canonical Option Contract and observation schema. This research does not select a provider. It defines the facts that a provider pilot and license review must prove.
